Introduction

Projectile motion describes the motion of an object thrown or projected into the air, subject only to gravity. This is one of the most fundamental concepts in physics and has applications in sports, engineering, and space exploration.

Key Concepts

  • Trajectory: The parabolic path followed by a projectile
  • Initial Velocity (v₀): The speed at which the object is launched
  • Launch Angle (θ): The angle at which the object is projected
  • Range: The horizontal distance traveled by the projectile
  • Maximum Height: The highest point reached by the projectile

Key Equations

Horizontal Position: x = x₀ + v₀·cos(θ)·t
Vertical Position: y = y₀ + v₀·sin(θ)·t - ½·g·t²
Range: R = (v₀²·sin(2θ))/g
Maximum Height: H = (v₀²·sin²(θ))/(2g)

Example Problems

Example 1: Basketball Shot

Beginner

Problem: A basketball player shoots a ball at 8 m/s at an angle of 60° from a height of 2m. How far does the ball travel horizontally before hitting the ground?

Show Solution

Given:

  • v₀ = 8 m/s
  • θ = 60°
  • y₀ = 2 m
  • g = 9.8 m/s²

Step 1: Find vertical component: v_y = 8·sin(60°) = 6.93 m/s

Step 2: Find horizontal component: v_x = 8·cos(60°) = 4 m/s

Step 3: Find time to hit ground using: y = y₀ + v_y·t - ½·g·t²

0 = 2 + 6.93t - 4.9t² → t ≈ 1.64 s

Step 4: Find range: R = v_x·t = 4 × 1.64 = 6.56 m

Answer: The ball travels approximately 6.56 meters horizontally.

Example 2: Maximum Range

Intermediate

Problem: At what angle should you launch a projectile to achieve maximum range? Prove your answer mathematically.

Show Solution

Given: Range formula R = (v₀²·sin(2θ))/g

Step 1: To maximize R, we need to maximize sin(2θ)

Step 2: The maximum value of sin(x) is 1, which occurs when x = 90°

Step 3: Therefore, 2θ = 90° → θ = 45°

Answer: A launch angle of 45° gives maximum range (assuming no air resistance and level ground).

Summary

What We Learned

  • Projectile motion follows a parabolic path
  • Horizontal and vertical motions are independent
  • 45° angle gives maximum range
  • Time of flight depends only on vertical motion

Key Takeaways

  • Break velocity into components (v_x and v_y)
  • Use kinematic equations for each direction
  • Gravity only affects vertical motion
  • Horizontal velocity remains constant

Real-World Applications

  • Sports (basketball, football, golf)
  • Artillery and ballistics
  • Space vehicle trajectories
  • Water fountains and sprinkler systems
